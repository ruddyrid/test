The Honda Civic,
decade by decade

A 2020 Civic costs about 2.5× a 2010, 3× a 2000, and 4× a 1990. But the curve isn't a straight line: the oldest cars have stopped depreciating — and clean ones are climbing. Here is what each decade actually sells for, what it originally cost, and how much car you get per dollar.

04How wide the spread gets

The older the car, the less the model year tells you and the more condition, mileage and rust decide the price. The 2020 trades in a tight band; the 1990 ranges from a $1,500 project to a $12,000+ original Si.

06What it means

Four decades, four very different value propositions.

🟢2020 — the expensive default

~$18,750. Still ~90% of sticker, ~77k miles. You're paying for a modern car with a full safety suite, CarPlay, and most of its life left. It is the rational daily driver — and the worst value per dollar of metal.

🔵2010 — the sweet spot

~$7,455. Fully depreciated but still a normal used car: airbags, ABS, decent crash safety, cheap parts. At ~139k miles, budget for timing belt/water pump, suspension bushings, and rust checks on rear arches.

🟡2000 — the enthusiast tax

~$6,200. A 26-year-old economy car asking nearly as much as a 2010. Clean EK/EM1 Si and manual coupes drive the premium; ordinary DX/LX sedans are $3,000–$5,000. Inspect for rust, timing belt, and abused mods.

🟣1990 — two markets in one

~$4,500 median. A $2,000 beater or a $10,000+ collector piece. EF-generation cars are now appreciating: originality, rust-free shells, Si trim and manuals command real money. As a daily, only if you enjoy wrenching.

The counter-intuitive part: the cheapest decade is no longer reliably the cheapest car. A rough 1990 is the least expensive way into a Civic, but a clean one can cost more than a 2000 or even a high-mileage 2010. Past roughly 25 years, condition and originality replace age as the price driver.
